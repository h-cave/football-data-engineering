# CB Press Model
## Model Philosophy
The key idea behind this model is that it rewards the quality and consequence of a centre-back's decision under pressure, rather than simply measuring whether they completed a pass. That is an important distinction and helps explain why the model is designed this way.

1. What does this model measure?

The CB Press Model awards a centre-back (CB) one point when they successfully complete a pass under pressure and the player receiving the pass subsequently completes a successful pass of their own.

The aim is to reward centre-backs for successfully playing out of pressure and helping their team retain possession, rather than simply rewarding the completion of a pass.

2. Why is a point only awarded when the recipient also completes a successful pass?

A point is only awarded if the recipient of the centre-back's pass subsequently completes a successful pass.

This is designed to avoid rewarding centre-backs for passes that put their teammates in difficult situations or leave them unable to retain possession.

For example, a centre-back might successfully complete a pass under pressure but play the ball to a teammate who is unable to receive it effectively, or who immediately comes under pressure they cannot escape.

By requiring the recipient to complete a successful pass, the model aims to distinguish between passes that genuinely help a team play out of pressure and those that simply transfer the pressure to another player.

In essence, the model is designed to avoid rewarding centre-backs for passing teammates into trouble.

3. How is playing time calculated?

Playing time includes:

Normal playing time.
Added time at the end of each half.
Extra time, where applicable.

This ensures that all relevant playing time is included in the model's calculations.

4. Current limitations and areas for development

There are several limitations to the current model that should be considered.

## Identifying progressive passes

The model does not currently distinguish or quantify how many progressive passes a centre-back completes under pressure. While it identifies successful outcomes from playing out under pressure, it does not measure the progression achieved by those passes.

## Limited range of successful outcomes

Currently, a centre-back only receives a point if the recipient's subsequent successful action is another pass.

This could be expanded to include other positive outcomes, such as:

The recipient scoring a goal.
The recipient successfully completing a cross.
Progressive yardage.
Other actions that represent a positive outcome from playing out under pressure.

These actions could reasonably be considered successful outcomes of the centre-back's initial pass and should therefore be considered for inclusion.

### Loss of possession followed by an immediate recovery

If the recipient loses possession but immediately wins the ball back before completing a successful pass, the centre-back does not receive a point under the current model.
